## Page 3 — #1 **WORTMANNS**

What can we say about #1? Good bad or stale ale it's still number one.
Kind of a dusty varnished atmosphere that reminds one of his uncles
redecorated basement (this turns out to be a reoccuring theme in Lynn)
done around 1965. What can you say, when watching (on the COLOR TV ) TV
and the bartender asks you if it's "Grease" by way of a conversation
starter, and it is in fact "Saturday Night Fever". What can you say?
Somehow appropriate for our first stop on our biblical trek - nay Crusade-
through Lynn. A "Special Place" and still number one in our book ar,ar.

[*Photograph: street view looking down a sidewalk. A KENWOOD one hour parking sign
and a DEAD END sign in the foreground; the Wortmanns sign and a Schlitz globe sign
project from the storefronts on the right.*]

## Page 4 — #2 **BOHILL'S**

For those of you who like draft Guiness , authentic live Irish music and
good food - this is the place in Lynn for you. Clean, nicely laid out bar
with pleasant decor and of course, an all Irish staff. A good place to
take the family, almost yuppie- but it's not crowded enough and the prices
are too reasonable. Deserves the Gold Star even at this early stage.

You could bring mom here. It's a good show - never stuffy.

[*Photograph: brick building, hanging sign in blackletter reading "Bohill's Irish Pub"
above a recessed storefront with pilasters.*]

## Page 5 — #3 **MR. MAX'S PUB**

A dark squarish bar with an inactive, slurring clientele. In fact, one
patron was found to be snoring next to his Miller. The frowning barmaid
seemed to be used to this type of behavior. She seemed refreshed when she
was able to talk with people that could speak (not just string together
vowels and syllables composed mostly of sour mash). Even the juke box
seemed tired and slurring. It was comforting to **leave** the men's room. We
were told "You can find a seat on a Friday or Saturday night", hard to
imagine anyone could stay away at all.

in front.*]

## Page 7 — #4 **39 BUFFUM STREET**

Now that's practicality for you: Why not name your bar by its address -
that way you can always find it!!

OK, OK, this place has a reputation, well earned I'm sure. But it's a
lively place and if you like darts and a lot of AC  DC with Angus Young on

It may even be the place for lunch - they've got specials. The softball

## Page 8 — #5 **THE COACHMAN LOUNGE**

What a stroke of luck - we hit the night that the French barmaid was

Grant had a "bad" Coors on tap, I had a Miller on tap. This was a turning

A real OK place.

## Page 11 — #8 **THE CRYSTAL GRILL**

If aquamarine bommer-rang formica is your scene, this place is for you.
The Crystal is a long, narrow bar with an exit at either end - not that
you'll want to leave - what, with Ernie serving up the brews, the color
TV, the 10 pm beer belly measurements (Ernie thought he had a 42" but it
topped out at an impressive 45") the dusty, yellowed Clydesdale's mode,
and bright, fluorescent lighting, there's atmosphere o'plenty here. So
curl up with your beer, it's plenty cold, and let Ernie tell you the bits
of wisdom he's gathered - Quote "I'm not a fast bartender, I'm not a slow
bartender, I'm a halfast bartender". Oddly, there's no Bud here. Only
Miller, Coors & Miller Lite. My bar stool had been ripped out of the
floor with only 4 bolt studs left in the floor. There was an older feel
to the crowd. I'm not sure, but I think they miss us.

A must see.

[*Photograph: very dark exposure; a lit letter "E" sign and a row of small
illuminated signs along a dark storefront.*]

The draft Moosehead was cool and crisp - duly impressed. You can't go

## Page 13 — #10 **CORCORANS**

We walked in. The woman across the bar gave us a look you could have
poured on a waffle. She looked like she liked waffles. We'd no more than
sat down to wet our whistles than the black guy next to me wanted to sell
me some blow. This is a good place to go drink without the needless
distractions of good food, decor and company. One of the patrons, who we
had met at Mr. Max's, had definitely gone into english optional and wanted
to talk to someone about something that had happened to him somtime ago at
someplace or the other and didn't this mean something to someone?

Jim was glad we finished our beer, before he tried to strike up a
conversation with either of us. A dark, tudor interior along with green
lights on the bottles arranged in the middle of the island style bar,
creates an atmosphere reminiscent of nausea.

## Page 14 — #11 **CRONIN'S CAFE**

A very special place, perhaps not the place, but the people. The
atmosphere was very calming, no anxiety here. Deja Vu: We have here the
identical Clydesdale model as we found in the Crystal!

But the real story here is not the structure - for the bar was simply four
walls - what made it special was **Skippy!**

We first saw Skippy as he proudly waddled into Cronin's with a stolen case
of Very Fine Grapefruit Juice. He promptly plopped the case on the bar
and stated "I,I,I, could gotten two #@*!%¢ cases of 'em - do you want one?"

The astute barmaid replied, "Don't you bring that stolen merchandise in
here Skippy.. All I need is the cops".

So obediently, Skip hoisted the hot grapefruit juice on his shoulder and
plodded toward the door. As he did so, his shorts lost their fight
against gravity. To which the Barmaid offered "Pull you Bermudah's up,
Skippy, or you're gonna get arrested for indecent exposah!"

But that wasn't all. As we were walking down the street, exiting
Cronin's, we happened to meet Skippy hurriedly escorting a case of Diet
Pepsi (tonic as he referred to it) on his shoulder down the sidewalk.

As we approached, Grant startled Skippy by saying, "Ah Skip, not another
case".

Just then, Skippy lost it all: The cardboard case folded in half which
sent Pepsi Cans careening to the ground and rolling down the street.

Skippy, obviously frantic, but intent on reassembly his case, blurted out
"You.... you guys made me do that!"

We laughed and walked on, reminiscing on Cronin's barmaid's words of
wisdom, when Skippy left with his case of Grapefruit Juice:

"They let 'em out on weekend passes....but they keep the wrong ones locked
up!"

## Page 18 — #14 **TONY'S CAFE**

Come on in, this place is on the street below "Rocky's Gym". An older
crowd graces this avid, older bar with painted tin ceilings (brown).

The ceilings were high and so was the elder clientele as one fine
gentleman sauntered over to the juke box to make such awesome rock & roll
selections as "Paper Roses" - Marie Osmond. Some slow dancing was present
but sitting was king.

The men's room entrance was unique and quite challenging, it narrowed as
you neared your destiny - no fat people made it into this restroom - I
guess they opted for the women's room.

The classic, vintage Clydesdales were present. But let's not forget that
Tony's offered fine cuisine: $1.00 sandwiches, your choice: Tuna **or** Egg
Salad!

## Page 19 — #15 **CONTI'S CAFE**

Walter is your bartender for the evening at this quaint bar located in the
heart of historic Lynn. It's sad but whenever urban renewel is mentioned,
it's places like this they want to put on the business end of a wrecking
ball.

There are booths here but these reviewers choose some open stools at the
bar. And what a bar!! If cheap beer is your thing,  then it's $1.10 Buds
will wet your whistle. Looks like maybe Conti's had some plans for some
remodelling of the back room, but this was closed off to the public by an
Army blanket. Although who knows, maybe they're planning a grand opening
gala! They also had a real old timer there who was nursing a brew at a
booth between periodically shuffling over to air his whiskers at the fan
near us. Someone finally showed him out the door. Oddly, they also have
an announcement for a dart team but no dart board - so leave em' at home.

Looks like the team is going to have to do a little fund raising.

[*Photograph: a brick side elevation with a fire escape, a projecting illuminated
sign reading "Conti's CAFE" with a clock face above it, cars parked along the kerb.*]

## Page 23 — #19 **THE CORINTHIAN CLUB**

Off the beaten path but you know what the saying is. When we first walked
in the "Members & Guests Only" sign sort of worried us, but they seem to
have an enlightened attitude about patrons. Bathroom is great with signs
along the walls & in front of you - "Flush Like You Mean It", "Stand
Close, Flush", "Flush, So I don't Smell", "Pretend You're At Home - Flush,
Flush, Flush". Even I got the point. People must get very drunk here.
While getting the beers, I met Jim Hughes who came to be our host for the
evening. Jim sported a red moustache and a white Miller Lite hat and was
obsessed with discussing the Commonwealth Status of the Eastern Seaboard
States. When I introduced Jim and he found out they had the same name, he
talked about how his dad's name was Jim but his mother called him Jamie,
his son was called Jimmy and he had a grandson James. I guess they like
the name. Jim was 47. He didn't remember how long he'd been a member but
told us the bar used to light up "all different colors" and "was any of us
good at wiring?...I'm a painter by trade so I know about colors but not
wiring.....You guys ought to go to the Lithuanian Club behind the Bus
Station...cause you can't go into all the bars in this town cause you're
not the right color. ("What's the right color Jim?" we asked). "What's the
right color? GREEN. You guys going? Well, bye Grant and Jim. Watch out
for the blue people."

"Thought you said the green people?"
"Oh, them too, but blues are the worst."
"Uh, O.K., bye Jim."

So ended our visit to the Corinthian.

## Page 26 — #22 **THE SHAWMUT GRILLE**

Enter in the rear of this one-story stucco building, reminiscent of a bomb
shelter; Welcome to the Shawmut. Its a hop back in time - thirty years or
more - a real Rod Serling gem.

Although the decor - tables, bar stools, waitresses, etc., were all
straight out of the 1950's, all were so well preserved, it was downright
eerie.

The atmosphere was lighthearted and full of dance while the Shawmut Trio
methodically rummaged through as many as three songs in a row between
breaks. The band consisted of a jock on the magnus, "we can make any song
sound corny" organ, a cat with brushes rapping on the skins, and an ace on
sax.

The average age of the Shawmut's guests was over 60, nevertheless, dancing
was king - a good place to trip the light fantastic. We spent time at one
of the Shawmut's many formica tabletop booths, which incidentally had juke
box selections at our fingertips. With choices like: "Hey Bartender" by
Jerry Lee  and "Under the Boardwalk" by (J.C.M.). The Shawmut was
unanimously voted the best of the evening!!

## Page 28 — #24 **BUCHANAN'S**

A landmark. Although this tiny bar is strategically located on the corner
of Eastern and Western Avenue, on a Saturday night there was plenty of
room for two strangers. The Buchanan atmosphere was warm and very
friendly, the regulars took to us right away and treated us as cousins.

The bartender asked us if we had I.D.'s. We said "Yes" and he said O.K.
and served us two Budweisers without seeing our licenses - what a trusting
soul. What a pal.

When visiting the Buchanan, one must take note of the many historic
pictures (patrons, golf teams, softball teams, bowling teams) on its walls
- showing that this bar has truly stood the test of time.

The men's room is very interesting: it has no sink, no urinal, no mirror;
it's just a room about the size of a small closet (you have to duck to
enter) with a 6 foot ceiling - with a toilet (with no tank) and a button
on the wall if you care to flush. Simple, functional, appropriate.

A good place to hoist em.

[*Photograph: a dark exposure down a street, a lit sign on a pole, parked cars.*]

> **ADDRESS, stated in the text:** "strategically located on the corner of Eastern
> and Western Avenue". The first explicit location in the document, and it supports
> the Buchanan Cafe candidate at 648 Eastern Ave.

## Page 29 — #25 **THE GOLDEN CIRCLE**

Here's a fun place. Here is where we encountered our first incident with
bar management (and lost) !

When we first came into the dark and sparsely populated bar-room, we
had'nt noticed the dart board opposite to our seats.

We promptly, upon noticing the opportunity, asked the barmaid if we could
play. Upon receiving a positive response, we proceeded to fumble and
bumble around looking only to light up our playing target. And just as we
discovered the "right switch", a gentleman and a lady approached us
(obviously, the owners of the establishment). The conversation went like
this:

THEM: "You can't play darts."

US  : "Sure we can, just watch.....Why not?"

THEM: "Uh, we don't play darts here."

US  : "Then why do you have a dart board?"

THEM: "Well, uh, uh, we don't play at night."

US  : "Then why do you have lights on the board? Winter Games, I
       suppose."

End of conversation.

Distraught, the two frustrated would-be dartsmen drained their beers and
stormed out.

the corner, barred or grilled windows.*]

## Page 31 — #28 **THE HALL OF FAME**
### (recently the Trolley Car)

This is Lynn's only true sports bar. Maybe that says it all.
We were carded here. The barmaid thought I didn't look my age.
Then she said "Jim you look like Ron Howard with the moustache."
Jim replied, "Anyone but him."
"You'd like to have his money though!",she said.
"He does",I lied.

## Page 45 — AFTERWORD/DISCLAIMERS

December 15, 1986

Narrowly defined, the goal of this tour was to do 40 bars in 40 nights.
It is not a complete work, rather we liken it to a watershed work designed
to encourage the reader to his or her own contributions to the
literature. We deliberately skipped some bars as too fancy, or simply
missed others. The Venice and the 50 Club come to mind. It is, at best,
a moment frozen (well, most of 'em are frozen - the Shawmut's petrified).
In time, years from now, some will surely have drained the last key and
rolled on to that great empty keg in the sky or have been remodelled to
suit the latest fashion. So, why'd we bother? Why'd we do it? Who cares?
We each have our own reasons:

"Because it's beer." &nbsp;–&nbsp; Grant.
"I can't remember." &nbsp;–&nbsp; Jim.

One thing we both want to know is why anyone would read it.

typewritten text.
