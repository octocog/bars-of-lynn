# 1986 addresses — research pass 1

Researched 2026-10-01 by web search only.

## The headline finding is a negative one

**The 1986 Lynn city directory is not digitized anywhere reachable online.**
Internet Archive holds Lynn directories for 1832 and 1851 only. HathiTrust has
nothing for the 1980s. Newspapers.com and GenealogyBank are paywalled. ABCC
historic licence ledgers are not online.

**33 of the 40 are UNKNOWN and will stay unknown until somebody sits down with the
physical 1986 Polk directory at Lynn Public Library.** That is the gate on the whole
map idea, and no amount of further web searching will move it.

### Hypotheses deliberately NOT promoted
- **BURKE'S 757 LOUNGE**: 757 Western Ave is a real address, a 1930 built commercial
  building, currently vacant, and the number matches the bar's name exactly. But **no
  document anywhere names Burke's at that address.** Strong lead for the library, not
  a finding.
- **THE 595 CAFE**: a "595 Cafe" is listed at **597** Summer St. The number mismatch
  actually undercuts the theory that these names encode addresses. Worth resolving,
  because if 595 Cafe was at 595 Summer St then Burke's 757 almost certainly follows
  the same rule.
- **THE COACHMAN LOUNGE**: "The Coachman, Inc." registered 1988 at 170–170A Broad St,
  which postdates the 1986 visit, and the officers do not obviously fit.

## Two things to reconcile

**Golden Circle address conflict.** This research gives the original Golden Circle at
**328 Broadway**. The separate current status research gives its successor, Rolly's
Tavern on the Square, at **338 Broadway**. These may be two different buildings, which
would fit the successor story (new owners took the licence to a different premises in
Wyoma Square), or one of the two numbers is wrong. Needs checking.

**Smuggler's Cove may have been brand new.** The Daily Item's 2018 piece says the pub
"was established in 1986". Murnane and Albert visited on 12 August 1986. If both are
right, they catalogued a bar that had only just opened, sitting in the same list as
places that had been there for decades. And it is the one whose building was torn
down in 2018.

## What actually worked, for whoever goes to the library

1. **Itemlive**, the Daily Item's site. The single best source. Searching it by each
   of the other 39 names, especially with words like "closing", "demolished",
   "after 40 years", is the highest value next step that can still be done online.
2. **Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth corporations database.** This
   produced the Golden Circle and Coachman records. It takes a direct name search
   that a search engine cannot perform, so somebody should sit and query it for
   "Bull Pen", "Corcoran's", "Cronin's Cafe" and the rest.
3. **Yelp CLOSED listings**, which are often the only place a defunct bar's street
   address survives online at all.

Not productive: Internet Archive, HathiTrust, NOBLE Digital Heritage (pre 1920s
buildings only), Reddit.
