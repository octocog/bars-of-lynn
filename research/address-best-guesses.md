# Best guesses at the 1986 addresses — a checklist for the library

Built from three things: the sourced research, the location clues in the document
itself now that it is fully transcribed, and the fact that **bars visited on the same
night were usually walkable from each other**, so one known address anchors its night.

Tiers are honest. Tier D means I have nothing, and inventing a street for those would
be noise dressed up as help.

## TIER C — street level guess only, from the night clusters

These are the nights where one address is known, so the others were probably within
walking distance. Treat as "check this street first", not as answers.

**Night of 25 August — anchored by Burke's at 757 Western Ave.**
Ziggy's (26), The Arena (27), The Hall of Fame (28), Ruthies Place (30), Tony's (31).
Six bars in one night is the longest crawl in the book, so they were close together.
**Try Western Ave first.** Caveat: the Ruthies photograph shows a dense downtown
shopping street with a Travel Agents International next door, which reads more like
central Lynn than outer Western Ave.

**Night of 17 August.** Side Street (13), Tony's Cafe (14), Conti's (15), Mel's (16).
Conti's is "in the heart of historic Lynn" and threatened by urban renewal. Mel's is
"a brisk walk from Conti's". Tony's Cafe is "on the street below Rocky's Gym".
**Find Rocky's Gym in the 1986 directory and you will probably place three of these.**
A Side Street Pub is listed at 33 Spring St, unverified.

**Night of 12 August — anchored by Smuggler's Cove at 826 Washington St.**
Coachman (5), Blue Note (6), Crystal Grill (8). **Try Washington St and around.**
A Blue Note Lounge is listed at 111 Sagamore St. A Coachman, Inc. was registered at
170 Broad St in 1988.

**Night of 7 August — anchored by 39 Buffum St.**
Mister Max's Pub (3) was the other stop. **Try Buffum St and its immediate corners.**

**Night of 10 September.** McDonough's (36), Roadhouse (37), Market Square Pub (38),
Four Winds (39). This one spans the city, so they drove. The Roadhouse photograph
shows a tall pole sign in a forecourt beside a **COLOR TILE store**, which is a
commercial strip. Finding where Color Tile traded in Lynn in 1986 places the Roadhouse.

## The best single lead in the whole document

Jim Hughes at the Corinthian tells them to go to **"the Lithuanian Club behind the Bus
Station"**. The Lynn bus station is a fixed landmark, the Lithuanian Club would have
been a licensed social club with records, and this may also explain **#34**, which the
index calls The Bus Stop and the entry calls The White Eagle. White Eagle is the Polish
national emblem, and the 595 Cafe's regulars spoke Polish. There was an Eastern
European cluster in Lynn and at least three of these bars sit in it.

## Four landmarks to look up first

Each of these places several bars at once, so they are worth more than any single
address lookup:

1. **Rocky's Gym** — places Tony's Cafe and probably its whole night
2. **Bennie's Pizza** — confirms Hogan's II continuity back to 1986
3. **Color Tile** — places the Roadhouse
4. **The Lithuanian Club / the bus station** — places the Corinthian's neighbourhood

## Two oddities to resolve while you are there

**#27 THE ARENA has no entry.** The index lists it, but the document runs straight from
#26 on page 30 to #28 on page 31. Either it was never written or a page is missing from
the Xerox. Worth asking whoever sent you the copy.

**Ruthie's had horseshoes set into the public sidewalk outside.** If that stretch of
pavement was never replaced, they may still be there. That is a findable, physical
trace, and it would make a very good photograph for the site.
